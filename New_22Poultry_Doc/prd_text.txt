Executive Summary 
This document presents comprehensive market research and problem validation for 
22POULTRY, a platform designed to transform India's small-scale poultry industry. 
Based on 2024-2026 market data and validated farmer economics, we have 
identified a critical market gap: small-scale poultry farmers (managing 1,000-10,000 
birds) lose 45-55% of their potential revenue to middlemen while institutional buyers 
(QSRs, restaurants, hotels) struggle to source consistent, traceable, quality poultry 
from fragmented small farms. 
Key Findings: 
•​
Market Size: India's poultry market valued at ₹2.6 trillion (2025), growing at 
13.8% CAGR to ₹8.4 trillion by 2034 
•​
Target User Base: 6 million+ people employed in poultry farming, with 70% 
being small-scale farmers 
•​
Core Problem Validated: Farmers receive only 45-55% of mandi price when 
selling through traditional middlemen 
•​
Buyer Demand Confirmed: Food service market growing from $51B (2024) to 
$124B (2033) at 10.3% CAGR, with QSRs desperate for consistent B2B 
poultry sourcing 
•​
Competitive Landscape: C2C agritech marketplaces have failed (funding 
dropped 95% from 2021 to 2024), but B2B institutional supply remains 
underserved 
This document deliberately excludes solution exploration to maintain focus on 
problem validation and opportunity sizing. Solution design will follow in Phase 2 after 
stakeholder alignment on problem definition.

Problem Statement 
Core Problem Definition 
Small-scale poultry farmers in India (managing 1,000-10,000 birds) face 
structural isolation that results in 25-40% lower income compared to their 
potential. This isolation manifests in four critical dimensions: 
1. Market Access Isolation 
Problem: Farmers lack direct access to institutional buyers and must sell through 
middlemen who extract 45-55% of the final selling price. 
Evidence: 
•​
Research from agricultural economics studies shows farmers receive only 
45-55% of mandi price on average, with the margin between mandi price and 
farmer price larger in rural districts (65%) than in peri-urban areas (47%) 
•​
Traditional middlemen provide essential services (credit, market knowledge, 
logistics) that farmers cannot access independently, creating dependency 
•​
For a farmer selling 2,000 broiler birds at ₹200/bird wholesale price (₹4 lakh 
gross), the middleman margin reduces farmer revenue to ₹1.8-2.2 lakh — a 
loss of ₹1.8-2.2 lakh per cycle 
2. Information Asymmetry 
Problem: Farmers lack real-time market price transparency, quality standards 
knowledge, and awareness of institutional buyer requirements. 
Evidence: 
•​
Tracked prices by farmers are significantly closer to the prices they receive 
(₹2.15/kg) than to actual mandi prices (₹4.77/kg), indicating limited awareness 
of true market rates 
•​
Mandi price variation is extreme year-over-year, with 87% of variation 
unexplained by seasonal or market-specific factors, making it impossible for 
farmers to forecast prices accurately 

•​
QSRs and institutional buyers require traceability, food safety certifications, 
and consistent sizing/quality — standards that small farmers don't know how 
to meet 
3. Capital Access Barriers 
Problem: Small farmers lack access to affordable working capital for inputs (feed, 
chicks, medicine) and must rely on high-interest informal credit or supplier advances 
that lock them into unfavorable supply relationships. 
Evidence: 
•​
Feed costs account for 70% of total poultry farming expenses, creating 
massive working capital needs 
•​
Formal loans for poultry farming (₹5-9 lakh typical farm setup) carry interest 
rates ranging from 7% (NABARD subsidized) to 26% per annum depending 
on lender, but many farmers cannot access formal credit 
•​
When farmers take crop advances from commission agents or traders, they 
become obligated to sell to the same middleman at pre-agreed prices, 
reducing bargaining power 
4. Network & Knowledge Isolation 
Problem: Farmers lack peer networks for best practice sharing, quality training on 
disease management, and access to technical expertise that could improve yields 
and reduce mortality. 
Evidence: 
•​
Contract farming models in Tamil Nadu and Maharashtra provide training and 
technical support to farmers, demonstrating that knowledge transfer improves 
productivity 
•​
Government extension services exist but are under-resourced, with large 
integrators providing superior training to their contract farmers 

•​
Mortality rates and disease outbreaks (avian influenza, Newcastle disease) 
disproportionately affect small farmers who lack biosecurity knowledge and 
vaccination protocols 
 

Why Existing Solutions Have Failed 
The agritech marketplace model collapse (2021-2024) provides critical insights into 
what NOT to build: 
C2C Marketplace Failure 
Data: Agritech marketplaces raised $490 million in 2021 but saw funding collapse to 
just $26 million in 2024 (95% decline). Major players like WayCool struggled with 
massive losses despite high valuations. 
Why they failed: 
•​
Farmers remain skeptical of digital platforms, preferring traditional middlemen 
who understand local agricultural cycles and provide crucial credit support 
•​
High operational costs from cold chain, logistics, and farmer acquisition made 
unit economics unsustainable 
•​
Consumer demand for direct farm-to-table purchasing was overestimated; 
traditional retail remains dominant (45% market share in 2025) 
•​
Platforms tried to eliminate middlemen entirely without providing equivalent 
value (credit, local knowledge, relationship-based trust) 
What IS Working: Contract Farming Model 
Data: Contract poultry farming is an emerging trend particularly in Tamil Nadu and 
Maharashtra, supported by government subsidies. The National Livestock Mission 
provides up to 50% of project cost (max ₹25 lakh) for farm establishment. This model 
empowers small farmers through input support, assured B2B retention, and training. 
Why it works: 
•​
Guaranteed buyer (integrator) provides certainty that farmers value over spot 
market volatility 
•​
Upfront input support (feed, chicks, technical advice) solves the working 
capital problem 

•​
Training and quality standards from integrators improve farmer yields and 
reduce mortality 
•​
Relationship-based model aligns with farmer preferences for trusted partners 
over anonymous digital platforms

Supply-Side Opportunity 
Target Farmer Segment 
•​
Total addressable market: 6+ million people employed in poultry farming in 
India 
•​
Small-scale farmers: 70% of contract broiler farmers manage flocks of 
3,000-10,000 birds, making this the dominant segment 
•​
Geographic concentration: Maharashtra, Tamil Nadu, Andhra Pradesh, 
Telangana, West Bengal, Karnataka account for majority of production 
•​
Production scale: India produces 140+ billion eggs and 4.5 million tonnes of 
broiler meat annually (2024) 
 

Demand-Side Opportunity: B2B Institutional Buyers 
•​
QSR/restaurant sector: Intensive growth of organized food service restaurants 
creating great incremental demand for poultry, with chicken as a widely used 
staple ingredient 
•​
B2B procurement acceleration: Institutional buyers, QSRs, and cloud kitchens 
signing agreements for hygienic, portioned, and traceable poultry products 
•​
Digital procurement: Startups like Captain Fresh digitizing procurement with 
real-time inventory, pricing, and fulfillment solutions that appeal to enterprise 
clients seeking predictability 
•​
Cold chain investment: Digitally integrated cold chain logistics allowing 
real-time temperature monitoring, reducing spoilage, enhancing safety, and 
boosting trust among institutional buyers 
Market Gap: The Missing Link 
Current situation: QSRs and institutional buyers source from large integrators 
(Suguna, Venky's, IB Group) or scattered informal suppliers. Small-scale farmers 
cannot compete because they lack aggregation, traceability systems, and logistics 
coordination. 
The opportunity: No platform exists to efficiently connect small-scale farmers 
directly to institutional buyers with: 
○​ Quality standardization and traceability 
○​ Aggregated consistent supply from fragmented small farms 
○​ Logistics coordination for 3x/week pickups 
○​ Credit/input support that aligns farmer and buyer incentives

User Stories & Acceptance Criteria 
The following user stories define the core jobs-to-be-done without prescribing 
specific solutions. Acceptance criteria establish measurable validation gates. 
Epic 1: Farmer Market Access 
User Story 1.1: Direct Buyer Access 
As a small-scale broiler farmer managing 2,000-5,000 birds, 
I want to sell my birds directly to institutional buyers (restaurants, hotels, 
QSRs), 
So that I can earn 70-80% of the retail price instead of 45-55% through 
middlemen, increasing my profit by 25-30% per cycle. 
Acceptance Criteria: 
•​
Given I have 2,000 birds ready for sale, When I connect with a buyer, Then I 
receive a price quote that is at least 10% higher than my current middleman 
rate 
•​
Given I complete a sale, When payment is processed, Then I receive funds 
within 48 hours of delivery (vs. 7-15 days from middlemen) 
•​
Given I make a sale through direct buyer channel, When I calculate my net 
income, Then I earn ₹15,000-25,000 more per cycle than selling via traditional 
middleman 
User Story 1.2: Price Transparency 
As a small-scale poultry farmer with limited market knowledge, 
I want to see real-time market prices for poultry in my region, 
So that I can negotiate better prices and know whether I'm getting a fair deal. 
Acceptance Criteria: 

•​
Given I open the platform, When I view market prices, Then I see current live 
bird prices from at least 3 different mandi/market sources in my district 
•​
Given I receive a price offer, When I compare it to market rate, Then the offer 
is within 5% of the published mandi price (preventing exploitation) 
•​
Given market prices fluctuate, When prices change by more than 10%, Then I 
receive a notification via WhatsApp or SMS so I can time my sales better 
User Story 1.3: Guaranteed Buyer Contracts 
As a farmer who invests ₹2.8 lakh in feed and inputs upfront, 
I want to have a guaranteed buyer committed to purchasing my birds before I 
start a new batch, 
So that I don't take on market risk and can plan my cash flow with certainty. 
Acceptance Criteria: 
•​
Given I have a buyer interested in my supply, When I sign an agreement, 
Then the buyer commits to purchasing a specific quantity (e.g., 1,500-2,000 
birds) at a pre-agreed price floor 
•​
Given my birds are ready for sale, When I notify the buyer, Then pickup is 
scheduled within 48 hours (no more than 3 days delay) 
•​
Given the buyer commits to purchase, When the pickup happens, Then the 
buyer accepts at least 95% of my birds (rejections <5% due to quality issues) 
 

Epic 2: QSR/Buyer Supply Reliability 
User Story 2.1: Consistent Quality Supply 
As a QSR operations manager needing 150 kg of chicken per week across 5 
outlets, 
I want to receive consistent, sized, fresh poultry from a reliable aggregated 
source, 
So that my menu quality remains consistent and I never run out of chicken 
during service hours. 
Acceptance Criteria: 
•​
Given I place a weekly order for 150 kg, When delivery arrives, Then 95%+ of 
birds meet my weight specification (1.5-2.0 kg live weight) 
•​
Given I have scheduled 3x/week deliveries (Mon/Wed/Fri), When delivery day 
comes, Then my order arrives within the 2-hour scheduled window at least 
90% of the time 
•​
Given I receive a shipment, When I inspect quality, Then rejection rate is <5% 
(spoilage, size issues, disease visible) 
User Story 2.2: Food Safety & Traceability 
As a restaurant owner subject to FSSAI inspections and food safety audits, 
I want to have complete traceability records for all poultry I purchase (farm ID, 
vaccination records, slaughter date), 
So that I can comply with food safety regulations and protect my customers 
from foodborne illness. 
Acceptance Criteria: 
•​
Given I receive a poultry shipment, When I scan/access the batch ID, Then I 
can see: farm location, farmer ID, bird age, vaccination dates, feed type, and 
slaughter date 

•​
Given an FSSAI inspector requests documentation, When I provide 
traceability records, Then all required fields are present and verifiable within 5 
minutes 
•​
Given a food safety incident (customer complaint), When I need to trace the 
source, Then I can identify the exact farm and batch within 15 minutes for 
recall purposes 
User Story 2.3: Cost Competitiveness 
As a cloud kitchen operator managing tight margins (8-12% net profit), 
I want to source poultry at 10-15% lower cost than large integrators, 
So that I can maintain competitive menu pricing while improving my gross 
margins. 
Acceptance Criteria: 
•​
Given I receive price quotes from both platform and integrator 
(Suguna/Venky's), When I compare rates, Then the platform price is 10-15% 
lower for equivalent quality and service 
•​
Given I purchase 150 kg/week, When I switch from integrator to platform, 
Then I save ₹3,000-5,000 per month in poultry procurement costs 
•​
Given I evaluate total cost of ownership (price + delivery fees + quality), When 
I calculate net cost, Then the platform delivers at least 8% total cost savings 
vs. status quo 
 

Epic 3: Platform Trust & Reliability 
User Story 3.1: Payment Reliability (Farmer Trust) 
As a farmer who has been cheated by buyers in the past (delayed payments, 
quality disputes), 
I want to receive guaranteed payment within 48 hours of delivery, 
So that I can pay my feed suppliers and workers on time without cash flow 
stress. 
Acceptance Criteria: 
•​
Given I deliver birds to a buyer, When quality inspection is complete, Then 
payment is initiated automatically within 6 hours 
•​
Given payment is initiated, When I check my bank account, Then funds are 
received within 48 hours for 95%+ of transactions 
•​
Given a quality dispute arises, When resolution is needed, Then I receive at 
least 85% of the agreed payment even in worst-case scenarios (protection 
against total loss) 
User Story 3.2: Support in Local Language 
As a farmer who speaks Marathi/Tamil/Telugu and has limited English 
proficiency, 
I want to access platform support and documentation in my native language, 
So that I can understand contracts, get help when needed, and use the 
platform confidently. 
Acceptance Criteria: 
•​
Given I need customer support, When I contact the platform, Then I can 
communicate via WhatsApp or phone in my regional language (Hindi, Marathi, 
Tamil, Telugu, Kannada) 
•​
Given I have a question, When I reach out for help, Then I receive a response 
from a local-language support agent within 4 hours during business hours 

•​
Given I need to understand terms and conditions, When I view contracts, 
Then all critical documents are available in both English and my regional 
language

Critical Assumptions to Validate 
These assumptions underpin the problem definition. If any prove false, we must 
revisit the strategy: 
Supply-Side Assumptions (Farmer) 

•​
Assumption 1: Farmers will switch from traditional middlemen if offered 
20-30% higher prices with guaranteed payment 
○​ Validation method: Pilot with 20-30 farmers; measure switching rate and 
satisfaction 
○​ Invalidation signal: <50% of farmers approached agree to try platform 
•​
Assumption 2: Small-scale farmers can meet QSR quality and 
consistency requirements with minimal training 
○​ Validation method: Run quality audits on first 50 farmer deliveries; 
measure rejection rate 
○​ Invalidation signal: >20% rejection rate by QSRs due to quality/sizing 
issues 
•​
Assumption 3: Farmers have basic smartphone literacy and can use 
WhatsApp-based interfaces 
○​ Validation method: User testing with 10-15 farmers from target segment 
○​ Invalidation signal: >40% cannot complete basic tasks even with phone 
support 
Demand-Side Assumptions (QSR/Buyer) 
•​
Assumption 4: QSRs/restaurants will buy from small-farm aggregators if 
traceability and consistency are guaranteed 
○​ Validation method: Interview 15-20 QSR procurement managers; present 
value prop and gauge interest 
○​ Invalidation signal: <60% express willingness to trial the platform 
•​
Assumption 5: QSRs can save 10-15% on procurement costs vs. 
integrators while maintaining quality 
○​ Validation method: Compare platform pricing to integrator rates across 10+ 
QSRs 

○​ Invalidation signal: Net savings <5% after accounting for quality/logistics 
issues 
•​
Assumption 6: Logistics coordination (3x/week pickups from 200+ farms 
to 50+ restaurants) is operationally feasible 
○​ Validation method: Pilot logistics in one metro for 2 months; measure 
fulfillment rate and cost 
○​ Invalidation signal: <85% on-time delivery or logistics cost >5% of GMV

Proceed to solution design and MVP build ONLY if all three gates pass: 
•​
✓ Demand validated: >60% QSR interest confirmed 
•​
✓ Supply validated: >50% farmer switching willingness confirmed 
•​
✓ Regulatory path clear: Licensing feasible within acceptable time/cost 
If ANY gate fails, revisit the problem definition and consider documented pivot 
options before investing in solution development.
