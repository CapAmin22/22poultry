Executive Summary 
Title: The 22POULTRY Thesis — An Investor-Grade Validation of a Digital Platform for 
India's Small-to-Medium Poultry Farmers 
India's poultry industry is the largest commercial livestock enterprise in the country and one 
of the fastest-growing animal-protein sectors in the world. As of 2024–25, the industry has 
crossed USD 30.46 billion in value (Expert Market Research, 2024) and INR 2,304–2,636 
billion (IMARC, Mordor Intelligence), with consensus forecasts projecting growth at an 
8.1–13.8% CAGR through 2034. India produced 149.11 billion eggs in 2024–25 (rising 80% 
from 82.93 billion in 2015–16, per BAHS/DAHD), and 4.5–5 million tonnes of broiler meat, 
making India the second-largest egg producer and fifth-largest broiler meat producer 
globally. Yet beneath this growth story lies a deeply fragmented small-farmer crisis that 
22POULTRY is positioned to solve. 
The Small Farmer is the Engine — and the Victim — of the Indian Poultry Revolution. 
According to FAO (2008, updated 2019) and the ICRIER–PARI study "Poultry Revolution in 
India" (Mehta & Nambiar, 2021), nearly 70% of all commercial poultry farmers in India are 
smallholders with flock sizes of 3,000–10,000 birds — the exact 22POULTRY target. Another 
20% are marginal (under 3,000 birds), and only 10% are large farmers. Across the country 
there are over 1 million broiler farmers, ~25,000 commercial layer farmers, and an additional 
~36 million backyard poultry rearers (One Health Poultry Hub India, 2024). This is one of the 
largest unserved digital-customer pools in Asian agriculture. 
Pain Points are Quantitatively Validated. Feed comprises 60–75% of total poultry 
production cost (CareEdge Ratings, 2024; USDA FAS, 2025), with maize (46% of feed mix) 
and soybean meal (28–30%) the dominant inputs. During the 2020–21 feed shock, soybean 
meal prices tripled from Rs 32,000/tonne to Rs 100,000/tonne (Ideas for India / SOPA data), 
and the per-tonne feed cost for layer/broiler birds rose from Rs 32,000 to Rs 48,000 in a 
single year, pushing thousands of small farms to shut. Even in "good" 2024, the Indian 
poultry industry's input volatility remained the leading risk identified by CareEdge. A 
landmark March 2025 Down to Earth/Punjab field study found that Venky's contract growers 
received only Rs 17.17 per bird while the integrator sold the same bird for Rs 191.17 — a 
91% middleman margin capture, and net returns were negative for small and medium 
contract farmers. Independent broiler farmers in Punjab also incurred negative net returns 
from market price volatility. Credit access is equally bleak: only ~30% of farmers access 
institutional credit, while 70% rely on informal sources at 24–60% interest, against 7% in 
priority-sector lending (Inc42 Agri-Fintech analysis, 2022). 
Real Farmer Voices Confirm the Stress. A Suguna contract grower in Coimbatore told the 
Asian Development Bank (ADB, 2021): "While other companies in the industry struggled to 
keep their contract farmers, Suguna did its best not to leave us… They gave us a minimum 
income during the crisis which enabled us to cover basic costs like electricity, something we 
could not have done without this financial help." Yet the same model excluded the majority 
who weren't contract growers. Forum testimony on Engormix (Dr Girish Kolwankar, Premium 
Chick Feeds, 2022) records: "Conflict between farmers and companies have been major 
challenge for contract broiler farming…Soybean meal which never went above Rs 45/- 

galloped to almost above Rs 90/- last year… Indian layer industry is almost on alternate raw 
material. Mostly surviving on broken rice." On the same forum, an anonymous Chittoor 
(Andhra Pradesh) farmer pleads: "I am a farmer of Premium Chicken in Chittor District, 
Andhra Pradesh. I am trying to get in touch with senior staff to escalate the issues and 
problems we are facing locally. However, I am unable to reach out to them." That single 
quote — a farmer with no channel to his integrator — is the 22POULTRY thesis in one 
sentence. 
Government Policy is Tailwind, not Headwind. The Animal Husbandry Infrastructure 
Development Fund (AHIDF), originally Rs 15,000 crore, has been expanded to Rs 29,110 
crore through FY 2025–26 (DAHD official portal); it covers 90% loans with 3% interest 
subvention for 8 years for poultry feed and processing infrastructure. The National Livestock 
Mission (NLM) provides up to 50% subsidy (max Rs 25 lakh) for rural poultry units. The 
Kisan Credit Card scheme has been extended to poultry farmers with up to Rs 1.6 lakh 
collateral-free credit (Bank of India, NABARD circulars, 2023–25). Digital India and 
BharatNet are delivering ~88.9% 4G village coverage and 77.8% 5G village coverage 
(Ookla, October 2025); rural data consumption has grown from 3.2 GB/month (2016) to 12 
GB/month (2023) per TRAI; 488 million rural internet users now exceed 397 million urban 
users (Kantar–IAMAI Internet in India 2024 report). The rails for a 22POULTRY-style 
platform are finally laid. 
Competitive White Space is Real. India has 1,000+ agritech startups (World Economic 
Forum), 735 of them funded (Tracxn, 2026), but virtually none are vertically dedicated to 
small poultry farmers. DeHaat (13 million farmers, ~INR 3,000 Cr FY25 revenue) is 
crop-focused and has not entered southern states or poultry. Stellapps (3.1 million farmers) 
is dairy-only. Aquaconnect/AquaExchange serve shrimp farmers. Eggoz ($20M Series C, 
June 2025) is a B2C egg brand that uses ~100,000 birds across deep-integration farms — it 
is a buyer, not a horizontal platform. Suguna (42,000 contract growers, 15,000 villages — 
Mordor 2025) and Venky's are integrators, not neutral digital platforms; field studies (Punjab 
2024, Down to Earth 2025) show they systematically extract value. No incumbent offers 
what 22POULTRY proposes: a neutral, all-in-one digital marketplace + training + 
financial-services + peer-network platform for the 1,000–10,000-bird independent 
poultry farmer. 
Unit Economics Validate the $58M Revenue Plan. A 5,000-bird broiler cycle in India today 
produces a realistic Rs 1.5–4.5 lakh net profit at best, with margins shaved by 30–40% to 
middlemen and integrators (Agri Farming field surveys 2024; Suguna case studies). A 10% 
improvement in farmgate price plus 5–8% feed cost optimization plus 3–5% mortality 
reduction (FCR 1.6:1 industry benchmark) is a credible 20–30% farmer income lift — and 
even at the bottom of that range, a 50,000-farmer Phase 2 base generating ~6 cycles per 
year at modest GMV per farmer easily clears the platform-revenue mathematics shown in 
Section 7. 
The Investment Window is Now. Indian agritech funding has compressed from a $817 Mn 
peak (2022) to $202 Mn in 2025 (Inc42), but the sector is forecast at $28 Bn market value by 
2030 (Inc42, 25% CAGR). Investors are explicitly seeking "focused, scalable models 
targeting governments and enterprises rather than just farmers" — i.e., precisely the 
multi-revenue-stream, B2B2C model 22POULTRY proposes. Avian-flu outbreaks (March 

2025 fatal H5N1 case in Andhra Pradesh; 602,000 birds culled in 8 outbreaks across AP in 
March 2025 per WOAH/Reuters) further raise the urgency of farmer training, insurance, and 
surveillance — modules already in the 22POULTRY architecture. 
Bottom line: This report establishes that the 22POULTRY thesis is anchored in a 
verifiable USD 30+ billion industry, with a clearly identifiable 1.0+ million small-farmer 
SAM, documented 30–40% margin extraction, ~70% credit-access gap, severe and 
recurring disease/feed shocks, validated rural digital readiness, supportive 
government policy tailwinds, and no horizontal poultry-specific incumbent. Each 
subsequent section provides defensible primary-source evidence — government statistics,

Section 1: Indian Poultry Industry Overview & Sizing 
1.1 Market Size & Growth Trajectory 
Three credible market-research bodies converge on the size and trajectory of the Indian 
poultry industry. Expert Market Research (2024) values the industry at USD 30.46 billion in 
2024, growing at an 8.10% CAGR to USD 66.37 billion by 2034. IMARC Group (2025) 
reports a slightly higher INR-denominated value — INR 2,636 billion in 2025, projected to 
reach INR 8,433 billion by 2034 at a 13.80% CAGR. CareEdge Ratings (October 2024) 
forecasts 8–10% revenue growth in FY 2025 alone with operating margins expanding 
180–220 basis points. Mordor Intelligence (2025) sizes India's poultry meat market 
specifically at USD 6.61 billion in 2026, growing to USD 7.85 billion by 2031 at 3.48% CAGR 
— and the poultry feed market at USD 18.0 billion (2025) reaching USD 25.25 billion by 
2030 (7.0% CAGR). 
Cross-validating these against official sources: APEDA (Government of India, 2024) and the 
Department of Animal Husbandry & Dairying's Basic Animal Husbandry Statistics (BAHS) 
2024 confirm 149.11 billion eggs produced in 2024–25 (up from 138.38 billion in 2023–24 
and 82.93 billion in 2015–16 — an 80% decade-long rise). Broiler meat output is 4.5–5.0 
million tonnes annually, with some industry sources (Agriculture Institute, citing CareEdge) 
reporting up to 9.77 million tonnes when including all poultry meat categories. India ranks 
2nd globally in egg production (recently overtaking China in select datasets) and 5th in 
broiler meat. The country exported USD 134–184.58 million worth of poultry products in FY 
2022–24 to 64 destinations, primarily Oman, UAE, Maldives, Indonesia, Sri Lanka, and 
Vietnam (APEDA, 2025). 
Between 2000–2020, poultry meat production grew at an annual average of 9.2% and egg 
production at 7.2% — vastly outpacing cereal production at 1.5%. As the Agriculture Institute 
notes, this growth rate "rivals even the celebrated Green and White Revolutions in India's 
agricultural history." 
1.2 Broiler vs. Layer Split 

The market is overwhelmingly broiler-dominated by volume: per Mordor Intelligence (2025), 
broilers account for 85.15% of poultry meat volume in 2025, with a 42-day grow-out 
cycle and a 1.6:1 feed conversion ratio. Duck consumption is below 5% (highest in West 
Bengal/Assam) but growing at 6.32% CAGR. Within the feed market, broiler feed leads at 
66% share, with breeder feed forecast to grow fastest at 9.2% CAGR through 2030. 
Per the IMARC poultry feed report (2024), the Indian poultry feed market alone was valued 
at INR 955.3 billion (2024), projected to INR 1,290.7 billion by 2033 at 3.23% CAGR — 
broilers account for the largest animal-type segment.

1.6 Contract Farming Penetration 
The Indian poultry sector is the most vertically-integrated commodity in Indian 
agriculture. ICRIER–PARI (2021) reports that in 2020, ~80% of India's poultry production 
(in value terms) came from organized contract farming — "far more than any other 
commodity in Indian agriculture." However, contract farming is heavily concentrated in 
southern and western India: in Punjab (a northern state), only ~50% of broiler farms are 
under contract with integrators (IB, Suguna, Venky, Sampoorna), 30% are independent 
private, and 20% are with local integrators (Punjab Agricultural University study, 2024). 
Suguna Foods is the largest single integrator: 42,000 contract growers across 15,000 
villages, 70 hatcheries, 60 feed mills, 4 processing plants (Mordor Intelligence 2025). 
Venky's, Skylark, Godrej Tyson, Sneha Farms, and IB Group make up the next tier. Together 
Suguna + Venky's + Skylark hold ~28% of poultry meat volume — the rest is fragmented 
across thousands of independent producers. This ~70% non-contract or 
weakly-contracted small farmer base is the 22POULTRY addressable market — 
farmers without integrator support, exposed to full input cost and price risk, with no formal 
aggregator. 
1.7 Industry GDP Contribution 
Per the Department of Animal Husbandry and Dairying Annual Report 2022–23 and the 
Agriculture Institute: poultry contributes ~1% of India's national GDP and ~14% of the 
livestock sector's GDP. The sector generates direct employment for over 1 million broiler 
farmers and 25,000 layer farmers, sustaining an even wider ancillary network in feed, 

hatcheries, veterinary services, cold-chain, and processing. The recent August 2025 launch 
of the Indian Poultry Alliance (Allana Group's USD 120 million / Rs 1,000 crore venture) 
achieving Rs 800 crore topline in its first year demonstrates that capital is now flooding into 
the organized side of the sector — making digital platform infrastructure for the unorganized 
side both urgent and timely. 
Section 2: The Small-Scale Farmer Reality — Pain 
Points with Data 
2.1 Pain Point #1: Middleman Margin Extraction — Validated at 30–91% 
22POULTRY's foundational claim is that small farmers lose 30–40% margins to middlemen. 
The empirical record is, if anything, more severe than the thesis assumes. 
Direct evidence from Punjab field study (Down to Earth, March 2025): A peer-reviewed 
primary survey of 50 Venky's contract growers and 50 non-contract farmers in Bathinda and 
Mansa districts (Punjab Agricultural University, published March 1, 2025) found that the 
integrator sold each broiler bird for Rs 191.17 and paid the farmer just Rs 17.17 — a 
margin retention of Rs 174 per bird, or approximately 91% of the gross sale value. On a kg 
basis, contract farmers received only Rs 9.15/kg against the integrator's sale of Rs 102/kg. 
The study concluded that "the welfare of broiler farmers under contract farming did not 
improve" and that broiler contract farming was being conducted "in a manner similar to 
'putting out work' or 'wage work'." 40% of contract farmers were small/marginal, vs. only 20% 
in non-contract. 
NECC data validates wholesale-to-retail markups: As of late 2025/early 2026, the NECC 
wholesale (farmgate-equivalent) egg price was approximately ₹5.53/piece (KisanDeals 
tracker). Retail prices simultaneously stood at ₹5.73–₹6.20/piece, and supermarket prices 
₹5.83–₹6.20 — a wholesale-to-retail spread of ~4–12%. However, this understates the 
actual price journey: NECC itself acknowledges that "the difference in the farmer's selling 
rate and the consumer's buying rate is 35–40% (the lowest in the world)" — meaning Indian 
farmers capture only 60–65% of consumer value. NECC's egg time-series via CEIC shows 
hyper-volatility — Hyderabad rates ranged from ₹176.57/100-piece (April 2009) to a record 
₹655.97/100-piece (December 2025), exposing small farmers without forward-contracts to 
extreme margin compression. 
Comparative context (Ashok Gulati et al., ICRIER): In dairy, farmers receive ~70% of final 
price; in eggs ~75%; in poultry meat farmers + aggregators together get ~56%. By contrast, 
vegetable farmers receive only 33% (tomatoes), 36% (onions), 37% (potatoes). Egg 
producers are relatively better-positioned than vegetable farmers but still surrender 25–44% 
of value to intermediaries. 
The "Asymmetric Information and Middleman Margins" experiment (Mitra et al., 
International Growth Centre / LSE, 2012): Direct field evidence from Indian agricultural 
producers showed that "low outside options of farmers prevent informational interventions" 
from reaching farmers — proving that information access alone (without market linkage) 

does not fix margin extraction. This is the precise architectural justification for 22POULTRY's 
combined marketplace + training + community design. 
Supermarket procurement (Andersson et al., ScienceDirect): Smallholder vegetable 
growers in India who sold to supermarkets received 20%+ higher farmgate prices than 
traditional channels, with the gap attributed to "fewer intermediaries and lower transaction 
costs." Applied to poultry, this directly underwrites the 22POULTRY hypothesis that digital 
direct-channel marketplaces can deliver 10–20% price gains. 
2.2 Pain Point #2: Feed Cost Volatility — 60–75% of Production Cost 
Feed dominates poultry economics. Multiple authoritative sources align on the share: 
●​ CareEdge Ratings (October 2024): "Feed costs, predominantly comprising maize 
and soybean meal, account for approximately 65–70% of total poultry production 
costs." Maize ~65–70% and soybean ~30% of the feed mix. 
●​ Vprint Infotech / Indian Feed Industry Analysis (2025): "Poultry feed typically 
represents 60–75% of the cost of broiler production." 
●​ NECC (e2necc.in) and Engormix industry forums: "Feed accounts for around 
75–80% of production expenses." 
●​ USDA FAS Grain & Feed Annual (2025): Commercial feed in India services poultry 
(75–78%), aquaculture (12–14%), and dairy (10–12%) — confirming poultry's 
dominance. 
The 2020–21 feed shock was catastrophic and instructive: 
●​ Soybean meal prices rose from Rs 35,100/tonne (Aug 2020) to Rs 
90,000–100,000/tonne in August 2021 — a tripling in 12 months (SOPA / Ideas for 
India data). 
●​ Maize wholesale prices rose 42% (Rs 11.45/kg → Rs 16.30/kg) in the same period. 
●​ Per the All-India Poultry Breeder Association, total feed cost rose from Rs 
32,000/tonne (Aug 2020) to Rs 48,000/tonne (Aug 2021) — a 50% jump. 
●​ The Business Standard (2019) had already reported maize prices rising 21% in two 
months to Rs 2,000/quintal, "cost of egg and broiler production gone up by 15–16%." 
●​ Ramesh Khatri, then-President of the Poultry Federation of India, told Business 
Standard: "Many farmers have already shut their shops due to viability issues. If the 
current trend continues, poultry farmers would be in trouble again." 
The 2021 crisis forced the Government of India to allow the import of 1.2 MMT of 
genetically-modified soybean meal — the first time in recent years — to rescue the poultry 
sector. Maize exports had grown 724% (0.35 MMT → 2.9 MMT) and soybean meal exports 
136% between 2019–20 and 2020–21, hollowing out domestic supply. Small farmers — 
without forward contracts, hedging tools, or bulk-buying power — bore the brunt. 
22POULTRY's marketplace + bulk-buying module is the direct antidote to this exposure. 
2.3 Pain Point #3: Credit Access — The 70% Gap 

The Inc42 Agri-Fintech analysis (2022) is unambiguous: "Approximately 30% farmers 
have access to institutional credit and balance 70% remain dependent on informal 
credit (annual interest rate ranging from 24 to 60% in informal credit against 7% under 
Priority Sector Lending). The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) for an average 
Indian farm (about 1 hectare size) continues to be over 20% per annum, because of heavy 
dependence on informal high-cost credit. There are not too many businesses in the world 
which can make money if the WACC is over 20%, leave alone the small holder farmers in 
India." 
Kisan Credit Card extension to poultry — under-penetrated: 
●​ DAHD launched a Special Drive (June–December 2020, extended several times) to 
bring 1.5 crore dairy farmers and 1.37 crore animal husbandry / fishery farmers under 
KCC. 
●​ As of last reported milestone: 53.10 lakh dairy applications collected, 45.75 lakh 
forwarded, only 25 lakh KCCs sanctioned for AH&D farmers (DAHD official data). 
●​ Bank of India and other PSBs offer poultry KCC with collateral-free credit up to Rs 
1.6 lakh (Pashu KCC: up to Rs 3 lakh max). 
●​ Interest rate: 7% baseline with 2% interest subvention (effective 5%) and additional 
3% rebate on timely repayment (effective 4%) — but only ~17% of eligible 
poultry/AH&D farmers have actually received the card. 
This is a textbook financial-inclusion gap, and the kind of digital-distribution problem 
fintech-on-agritech models (a la DeHaat, Stellapps MooPay) are positioned to solve. 
22POULTRY's Financial Services module addresses this directly. 
2.4 Pain Point #4: Disease Outbreak Losses — HPAI Recurrence 
Avian influenza is the systemic mortality risk for the sector. Per the Indian Journal of Medical 
Research (2023) and gavi.org reporting: 
●​ Since 2006 (first Indian outbreak in Navapur, Maharashtra), India has reported 
HPAI poultry outbreaks every single year. 
●​ Cumulative culling exceeds 9 million birds between 2006 and 2021, with 
Maharashtra, West Bengal, Kerala, and Odisha each recording 25+ outbreaks. 
●​ Kerala 2024 (Alappuzha, January): 38,000 birds culled, 10-km movement 
restrictions imposed (Mordor 2025). 
●​ Andhra Pradesh, March 2025: 8 HPAI outbreaks reported to the World Organisation 
for Animal Health, leading to death or culling of 602,000 birds (Reuters / WOAH 
report). 
●​ A 2-year-old child died of H5N1 in Mangalagiri, Andhra Pradesh, March 15, 2025 
(CIDRAP / Press Trust of India) — adding zoonotic urgency. 
●​ Compensation falls short: Under the Livestock Health and Disease Control 
Scheme, the Centre and States share 50:50 the cost of compensating culled birds, 
eggs, and feed. However, retail demand typically contracts 15–20% in affected 
regions for 4–6 weeks (Mordor), meaning even uninfected nearby farms lose 
revenue. 

The 22POULTRY Training and Networking modules — pushing biosecurity SOPs, disease 
alerts, and peer-shared early-warning signals — map directly to DAHD's "National Action 
Plan for Prevention, Control and Containment of Avian Influenza 2021" guidance. 
2.5 Pain Point #5: Training & Extension Gap 
The MEAS-Illinois evaluation study (2015) of contract broiler farming in India identified the 
extension gap precisely: "the EAS providers were trained supervisors but not poultry 
veterinarians. Veterinarians from the contract company visited the contract farms only 
in case of disease outbreak or when unusual mortality was reported." For non-contract 
farmers, EAS comes from "private poultry consultants, self-service and government 
veterinary doctors on payment" — i.e., out-of-pocket, unaffordable, and sporadic. 
The ICAR system has substantial research depth (Central Avian Research Institute, 
Izatnagar 1979; Directorate of Poultry Research, Hyderabad 1988; multiple state veterinary 
universities) — but reaching 1+ million small farmers through traditional KVK (Krishi Vigyan 
Kendra) workshops is structurally impossible. Each KVK covers a district, and there are 731 
KVKs nationally — that is ~1,400 farmers per KVK per district per year if all were dedicated 
to poultry, which they are not. Digital training delivery (22POULTRY's Training module) is 
not a luxury — it is the only architecturally feasible channel to scale agronomic and 
veterinary best practices to a population of this size. 
2.6 Pain Point #6: Price Volatility & NECC Cartel Risk 
NECC ("National Egg Coordination Committee") declared its first egg price in 1982. As of 
2022, it had 25,000+ farmer members and set zonal prices via WhatsApp and phone 
coordination. In 2022, the Competition Commission of India ruled against NECC for 
cartel-like price fixing — including discouraging members from selling below NECC rates, 
urging early flock culling to artificially reduce supply, and informal penalties (Finshots 
analysis, 2024). NECC was forced to label its prices "advisory" but, as Finshots notes, "when 
25,000 farmers in a concentrated sector follow the same platform, 'suggested prices' 
naturally become de facto market prices." 
This is a regulatory headwind that creates exactly the space for a neutral, transparent, 
real-time digital pricing layer — the 22POULTRY marketplace.

4.5 Why None Have Solved the Indian Small-Scale Poultry Problem 
Synthesizing the competitive landscape: 

1.​ Poultry-specific players (Suguna, Venky's, Skylark, Eggoz, Indian Poultry 
Alliance) are buyers/integrators with structural conflicts of interest. They extract 
margin from farmers; they cannot credibly be neutral platforms. 
2.​ General agritech players (DeHaat, Ninjacart, AgroStar) are crop-focused. 
Poultry has a wholly different lifecycle (35–42-day broiler cycle; 72-week layer cycle), 
input matrix (feed not seeds/fertilizer), disease profile (HPAI not crop pests), and 
price discovery mechanism (NECC daily quotes). Crop-platforms cannot simply add 
a poultry module without dedicated domain capability. 
3.​ Dairy/aqua platforms (Stellapps, AquaExchange) prove the model works for 
non-crop livestock, but they have not entered poultry — and the closest poultry 
"platform" (Eggoz) is a brand, not a horizontal platform. 
4.​ The funding winter has knocked out the "go-broad" agritech thesis ($202 Mn 
2025 vs. $817 Mn 2022 — Inc42). Investors now demand focused, vertical, 
narrow-segment plays with clear unit economics. 22POULTRY's narrow vertical 
focus aligns with the post-2024 capital market thesis rather than the pre-2022 
"horizontal everything" approach that has failed. 
The market gap is structurally defensible: a neutral, all-in-one digital platform for the 
1,000–10,000-bird independent Indian poultry farmer, in the priority southern + 
western + northern poultry states, does not exist. 22POULTRY is the first explicit 
attempt. 
Section 5: Government Schemes & Policy Tailwinds 
5.1 Animal Husbandry Infrastructure Development Fund (AHIDF) — Rs 
29,110 Cr 
Originally launched at Rs 15,000 crore in 2020 under the Atma Nirbhar Bharat Abhiyan, the 
AHIDF has been expanded to Rs 29,110.25 crore through FY 2025–26 by subsuming the 
Dairy Processing Infrastructure Development Fund (Department of Animal Husbandry and 
Dairying official portal, ahidf.udyamimitra.in). 
Key terms relevant to 22POULTRY's marketplace and financial-services modules: 
●​ 90% loan coverage of project cost from any scheduled bank, NABARD, NCDC, 
NDDB, or SIDBI 
●​ 3% interest subvention for up to 8 years 
●​ 2-year moratorium on principal, then 6-year repayment 
●​ Rs 750 crore Credit Guarantee Fund managed by NABARD (NABSanrakshan 
Trustee Pvt. Ltd.), providing 25% guarantee on loans for MSMEs 
●​ Eligible: FPOs, MSMEs, Section 8 companies, private companies, individual 
entrepreneurs with 10% margin money contribution 
●​ Online application portal at ahidf.udyamimitra.in 
●​ Maximum project loan: Rs 100 crore per project 
Use cases relevant to 22POULTRY-onboarded farmers: poultry feed plant establishment, 
animal feed plants, meat processing including sheep/goat/poultry/pig/buffalo in rural and 

semi-urban areas. The 22POULTRY platform can directly facilitate farmer access to AHIDF 
loans, serving as both an aggregator of demand and a documentation/Detailed Project 
Report (DPR) preparation channel. 
5.2 National Livestock Mission (NLM) — Realigned 2021–22 
The realigned NLM offers, among other entrepreneurship development components, 50% 
subsidy up to Rs 25 lakh per project for establishment of rural poultry farms, hatcheries, 
and brooder units (Expert Market Research, 2024; PIB releases). Targets: 
●​ Employment generation 
●​ Per-animal productivity enhancement 
●​ Increased meat, egg, milk, wool, fodder, and feed production 
5.3 Kisan Credit Card (KCC) for Animal Husbandry & Poultry 
Originally launched 1998 and extended to animal husbandry/fisheries in 2018–19. Key 
features (Bank of India, Bajaj Finserv, Krishi Jagran sources): 
●​ Eligibility: Individual poultry farmers, joint borrowers, Joint Liability Groups, Self Help 
Groups, including tenant farmers with owned/rented/leased sheds 
●​ Collateral-free up to Rs 1.60 lakh (Rs 3 lakh under Pashu KCC in some states) 
●​ Interest rate: 7% baseline, 2% interest subvention (effective 5%), additional 3% 
rebate for timely repayment (effective ~4%) 
●​ Special drive: 1.37 crore KCC target for AH&D farmers; only ~25 lakh sanctioned as 
of last reporting milestone — leaving an addressable gap of ~1.12 crore 
poultry-and-allied farmers who have not yet received the card 
This is the single largest financial-inclusion gap in Indian poultry today, and 22POULTRY's 
Financial Services module is architected precisely to close it via document digitization, KYC, 
and bank-app integrations. 
5.4 Pashu KCC (State-Level Animal Husbandry KCC Variants) 
Haryana, Bihar, and several states have launched Pashu Kisan Credit Card schemes with: 
●​ Max Rs 3 lakh loan (Rs 40,783 for cow, Rs 4,063 for goat/sheep, Rs 
720/chicken-laying hen — these are unit-of-measure ceilings, not totals) 
●​ 4% effective interest (7% baseline + 3% central rebate) 
●​ Collateral-free up to Rs 1.60 lakh 
●​ Aim: Bringing all 16 lakh-plus livestock-rearing families under formal credit (Haryana 
state model) 
5.5 Pradhan Mantri Mudra Yojana (PMMY) for Animal Husbandry 
Three categories of micro-credit available to small poultry farmers and entrepreneurs: 
●​ Shishu: up to Rs 50,000 
●​ Kishore: Rs 50,000 – Rs 5 lakh 

●​ Tarun: Rs 5 lakh – Rs 10 lakh 
This is the entry-level fund for smallholder poultry units and a natural fit for 
22POULTRY-onboarded marginal farmers (≤1,000 birds aspirants and 1,000–3,000-bird 
operators). 
5.6 Livestock Health and Disease Control Programme (LHDCP) & Pashu 
Aushadhi 
LHDCP provides 50:50 Centre–State funding for compensating poultry farmers for culled 
birds, destroyed eggs, and feed during HPAI/bird-flu outbreaks (DAHD policy framework, 
April 2025 PIB release). Pashu Aushadhi delivers affordable generic veterinary medicines 
including ethno-veterinary medicines through PM-Kisan Samriddhi Kendras (PM-KSKs) and 
cooperative societies — a national distribution network of veterinary input retail stores that 
can naturally integrate with 22POULTRY's marketplace. 
5.7 Poultry Venture Capital Fund (PVCF) — NABARD Legacy Scheme 
NABARD's older Poultry Venture Capital Fund has subsidized commercial poultry units. 
Replaced/subsumed in many respects by the realigned NLM Entrepreneurship Development 
component, but the underlying NABARD machinery for poultry refinance remains active. 
NABARD disbursed Rs 1,88,882.73 crore of short-term refinance in FY 2024–25, of which 
Rs 39,102.85 crore was concessional refinance under STCRCF and STRRBF (NABARD 
Annual data). 
5.8 State-Specific Poultry Policies 
●​ Andhra Pradesh: Largest poultry state; Suguna's contract-farming hub; Andhra 
Pradesh has long been a poultry-policy leader (Source: Murthy & Madhuri NAARM 
Suguna case study). State-level disease compensation schemes operate in 
coordination with LHDCP. 
●​ Telangana: Geographic spillover from AP; shares similar farming practices; high 
biosecurity enforcement. 
●​ Tamil Nadu: Namakkal zone is one of only two egg-zones in India (the other being 
Barwala near Delhi); 699 poultry farmers own ~4.01 lakh layer birds in this zone 
alone. State has facilitated contract broiler farming since the early 1990s. 
●​ Kerala: Kudumbashree (state's women's SHG program) launched the Kerala 
Chicken Project — December 2024 commercial launch of branded value-added 
chicken products (Drumsticks, Boneless Breast, Biryani Cut) with a processing plant 
in Kadinamkulam and mini unit in Anayara, Thiruvananthapuram (Mordor 2025). 
Demonstrates state-led women-centric poultry entrepreneurship — directly aligned 
with 22POULTRY's potential SHG outreach. 
●​ Maharashtra: Largest poultry feed market; significant Pune-Nashik integrated 
operations. 
●​ Haryana, Punjab: Pashu KCC and Animal Credit Card schemes most developed in 
these states. 
5.9 Disease & Biosecurity Policy 

The April 4, 2025 DAHD high-level meeting (PIB release ID 2119198) established a 
three-pronged Avian Influenza strategy: 
1.​ Stricter biosecurity measures — enhanced hygiene, controlled farm access 
2.​ Strengthened surveillance and MANDATORY REGISTRATION of all poultry 
farms with state animal husbandry departments 
3.​ Permission for H9N2 (Low Pathogenic AI) vaccination developed by 
ICAR-NIHSAD Bhopal; ongoing evaluation of HPAI vaccine 
The mandatory registration mandate is a structural tailwind for 22POULTRY: every 
Indian poultry farm will need a digital registration, and a farmer-facing app that handles this 
registration becomes a default-acquisition rail. India follows a strict "detect and cull" policy 
with 1-km culling radius around outbreak zones — alerts and biosecurity training delivered

7.7 Unit Economics Stress Test 
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC): Indian agritech CAC for farmer onboarding has 
historically run Rs 200–500 per acquired farmer via field agents. With the mandatory 
poultry farm registration mandate (DAHD April 2025) acting as a default acquisition rail, 
22POULTRY can target a sub-Rs 200 CAC. 
Lifetime Value (LTV): A small poultry farmer running 5,000 birds and transacting through 
22POULTRY for 5 years generates: 
●​ Marketplace fees (4%): Rs 45 lakh GMV/year × 4% × 5 years = Rs 9 lakh 
●​ Training subscription (Rs 1,200/year): Rs 6,000 
●​ Financial commission (Rs 1.6 lakh KCC + insurance + feed financing): Rs 
3,000–10,000/year × 5 = Rs 15,000–50,000 
●​ Total per-farmer 5-year LTV: Rs 9.5–10 lakh per active engaged farmer 

LTV / CAC > 100× — even with aggressive churn and platform-CAC assumptions, the unit 
economics are healthy. 
Conservatively scenario-tested with 70% churn and 50% lower realization, LTV/CAC 
still exceeds 25× — comfortably above the 3× minimum threshold for 
venture-backable consumer marketplaces. 
Section 8: Case Studies & Global Analogs 
8.1 DeHaat — The Definitive Indian Analog 
DeHaat (founded 2012 by Shashank Kumar and co-founders from IIT Delhi, IIT Kharagpur, 
IIM Ahmedabad) is the most important strategic case study for 22POULTRY because it has 
validated, at scale, every architectural choice 22POULTRY proposes — but in crops rather 
than poultry. 
The DeHaat journey: 
●​ 2012: Founded in Bihar — chose eastern India because of fertile belt + neglect of 
smallholder farmers 
●​ 2018: 220,000+ farmers; 520+ last-mile service providers ("DeHaat Centers" / 
franchisees) (IIIC newsletter) 
●​ 2021: 650,000+ farmers; Series D of $115M led by Sofina and Lightrock 
(TechCrunch) 
●​ 2022: $700–800 Mn valuation; Series E of $60M; 1.5 Mn farmers; 11 states; 
110,000+ villages; 10,000+ micro-entrepreneurs (TechCrunch / Tracxn) 
●​ 2025 (FY25): 13 Mn farmers post-AgriCentral acquisition; Rs 3,000 Cr revenue (up 
11%); Rs 200 Cr venture debt from Trifecta; targeting full-year profitability FY26 
What worked: 
1.​ Phygital model: 25–35 franchisees managed by 1 relationship manager → trust + 
scale combined 
2.​ Franchisee community membership: Franchisees are local; farmers see them as 
community members not corporates 
3.​ Crop-agnostic, year-round value: Avoids seasonal dropout 
4.​ No advisory fees; revenue from market linkage: Aligns success with farmer 
outcomes (CEO Shashank Kumar) 
5.​ Revenue mix: ~72% from market linkage of farm produce, balance from agri-input — 
validating marketplace as the primary revenue driver 
6.​ Inorganic acquisitions to add capability: AgriCentral (Olam Agri-backed advisory 
platform, Jan 2025) added 10M farmers and digital services suite 
7.​ Patient capital: 13 years to scale; agritech needs that horizon 
Lessons for 22POULTRY: 
●​ The DeHaat model proves a multi-state, multi-service platform with phygital last-mile 
can reach 13M farmers — even greater than the entire 22POULTRY long-term TAM 

●​ 22POULTRY's narrower vertical depth (poultry-specific) should produce better unit 
economics than DeHaat's crop-broad approach 
●​ Patient capital alignment is essential; the path to break-even is 6–8 years not 2–3 
●​ M&A to add capability (e.g., acquiring a regional poultry-feed marketplace, a 
veterinary content platform, or a state-level KCC origination engine) is a proven 
scaling lever

●​ Digitises 13M+ litres of milk daily worth $3.4M/day

○​ SmartMoo: IoT for milk production, procurement, cold chain

○​ MooGrow: Hyperlocal agro-inputs distribution

8.3 Suguna Foods — The Aggregator Success (and Caution) 
Suguna (founded 1984, Coimbatore, by Soundararajan brothers; pioneers of Indian poultry 
contract farming): 
●​ Started with 200 layer birds (Udumalpet, 1986) 
●​ Crisis pivot 1989–90: when oversupply caused price crash and indebted farmers 
couldn't pay; Soundararajans began providing inputs in exchange for end-product → 
birth of contract model 
●​ Initially could not get bank credit; no bank lent to Suguna until 1998; today 10+ 
banks compete 
●​ 2008 milestone: 15,000 contract growers, 10 states (BusinessToday) 
●​ 2025: 42,000 contract growers, 15,000 villages, 70 hatcheries, 60 feed mills, 4 
processing plants (Mordor) 
●​ Largest broiler producer in India; fourth-largest in world 
●​ DelFrez retail chain: expanding from 250 to 1,000 stores by 2028 
Lessons for 22POULTRY: 
●​ Positive: 1+ million Indian small farmers WANT structured value-chain participation 
— Suguna proved it 

●​ Negative: The 91% margin extraction documented by Down to Earth (2025) shows 
that integrator power can be corrosive — leaving a structural niche for a neutral 
platform 
●​ Strategic: 22POULTRY can partner with integrators (acting as digital infrastructure 
for their contract farmer base) OR compete (giving independent farmers tools to 
negotiate) 
8.4 Anand Model (Amul) — The Cooperative Lesson 
The Anand cooperative dairy model (Verghese Kurien, GCMMF/Amul, 1946 onwards) 
demonstrated that: 
●​ Smallholders can collectively bargain at scale 
●​ Trust + governance + technology can flip the buyer-power equation 
●​ ~1.5 crore dairy farmers organized across 230 Milk Unions today 
Lesson for 22POULTRY: The Cooperative DNA is in Indian poultry too — NECC (1982 
onwards) is the poultry analog of Amul. The opportunity is to build a digital cooperative in the 
poultry context, replicating Amul's bargaining-power benefits without the institutional inertia.

9.1 Disease Outbreak Risk — High Probability, High Severity 
The risk: HPAI (H5N1, H9N2) outbreaks occur every year in India since 2006. Cumulative 
documented impact: 9 million+ birds culled (Indian Journal of Medical Research 2023). 
Specific recent incidents: 
●​ Kerala 2024 (January): 38,000 birds culled in Alappuzha 
●​ Andhra Pradesh 2025 (March): 8 outbreaks reported to WOAH, 602,000 birds 
dead or culled (Reuters) 
●​ AP March 15, 2025: First Indian H5N1 human fatality in 4 years (2-year-old girl, 
Mangalagiri) 
●​ 2024–25 detections across Tamil Nadu, AP, Bihar, Maharashtra, Jharkhand, Odisha, 
Telangana, Chhattisgarh 
●​ Demand contraction: Retail demand drops 15–20% in affected regions for 4–6 
weeks (Mordor 2025) 
Mitigation through 22POULTRY: 
●​ Training module delivers DAHD-mandated biosecurity SOPs to farmers' phones 
●​ Networking/Social feed enables peer early-warning ("I'm seeing unusual mortality" 
→ community alert) 
●​ Financial Services facilitates LHDCP claim filing and avian influenza insurance 
enrollment 
●​ Marketplace can pivot to alternative regional buyers when local zones are 
quarantined 
●​ Risk cannot be eliminated, but 22POULTRY converts catastrophic farm shutdown 
into manageable single-cycle disruption with insurance backstop 
9.2 Climate Risk — Heat Stress, Water, Monsoon Variability 
Per industry research (AgriFarming 2024): 
●​ Summer (March–June): Heat stress raises broiler mortality, lowers feed intake, 
worsens FCR 
●​ Monsoon (July–September): Humidity → Ranikhet (Newcastle) disease, infectious 
bronchitis, coccidiosis 

●​ Water availability for poultry sheds is increasingly constrained in semi-arid regions 
Mitigation: Training content adapted by season; partnership with vaccine companies for 
region-specific protocols; weather-API integration in dashboard. 
9.3 Feed Price Volatility — Documented Historical Pattern 
The 2020–21 shock (soybean meal Rs 35,100 → Rs 100,000/tonne; maize +42%) 
demonstrated catastrophic exposure. The August 2021 cost spike forced ~1.2 MMT GM 
soybean meal import (first time in years). 2024 saw price stabilization (CareEdge), but 
2025–26 risks remain elevated due to global commodity flows. 
Mitigation through 22POULTRY: 
●​ Bulk-buying aggregation reduces small-farmer feed cost 5–10% 
●​ Forward-booking via platform when supply contracts emerge 
●​ Training on alternative feed ingredients (broken rice substitution, full-fat soybean per 
Nelson Ruiz forum advice) 
●​ Real-time mandi-price API integration so farmers can time purchases 
9.4 Contract Farming / Integrator Conflict 
If 22POULTRY succeeds, it competes with Suguna, Venky's, Skylark, IB Group, IPA, Sneha 
Farms — players with deep pockets and entrenched farmer relationships. 
Mitigation: 
●​ Position as complementary to integrators rather than confrontational — offer the 
platform to integrators as a digital infrastructure layer for their own contract networks 
●​ Focus initial Phase 1–2 on independent farmers (30% of broiler farms in Punjab; 
higher % in some states) who are not contracted 
●​ Maintain neutrality; do not become a buyer 
9.5 Regulatory & Compliance Risks

●​ State variations: Andhra Pradesh, Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Maharashtra, Telangana

●​ Antitrust precedent: NECC's 2022 CCI ruling against price coordination is a

●​ Data protection: India's DPDP Act (2023) requires KYC/farmer data handling
